## 1. Local market size — Philippine MSMEs

**1,241,476 registered business establishments; 99.63% are MSMEs (1,236,908).**
Only 0.37% (4,568) are large enterprises.

Breakdown:
- Micro: 90.66% (1,125,476)
- Small: 8.60% (106,799)
- Medium: 0.37% (4,633)

*Source: DTI, 2024 Philippine MSME Statistics —
https://www.dti.gov.ph/dti-knowledge-hub/dti-statistics (data: PSA List of
Establishments). A 2023 PSA briefing gives a near-identical 1,241,733 total.*

**Economic weight.** MSMEs comprise ~99.5% of Philippine enterprises, contribute
~35.7% of total value added (2020), ~60% of exports, and support ~62.7% of total
employment. (PSA 2020, cited in UNIDO 2022 *Outlook and Trends for Philippine
MSMEs*.)

**How to use this.** It establishes that the target population is over 1.2
million establishments and that even a very small share is a viable business.
It also frames the tool as *mainstream*, not niche — nearly every Philippine
establishment is an MSME.

### The distribution channel already exists: 40 Negosyo Centers

This is the most useful finding in the file for the venture model.

- DTI operates **40 Negosyo Centers in Cebu.** The 40th opened in **Cebu City on
  May 15, 2026**, in partnership with the Cebu City Government, bringing
  registration assistance, mentoring and financing information directly to
  entrepreneurs. DTI Cebu Provincial Director: Marivic Aguilar.
- The 36th (Cebu Province) opened in **Compostela, Aug 2024**; the 37th in
  **Carmen, Cebu, Nov 2024** — a town with nearly 2,000 registered businesses.
- Cebu also has the provincial government's **Sugbo Negosyo Center** program.
- Nationally, Negosyo Centers covered **1,311 LGUs in 2023 — 80.3% of all 1,634
  LGUs** — and government has committed to opening Centers in the remaining 323
  municipalities.

*Sources: DTI / SunStar Cebu; DTI Negosyo Center program pages.*

**Why this matters more than any statistic.** The B2B model in §3a proposed
licensing to DTI Negosyo Centers as the primary channel. It turns out the
channel is not hypothetical: **40 Centers are already operating in Cebu, doing
exactly the advisory work this tool equips, and the newest one is a DTI–Cebu City
Government partnership** — the precise institutional relationship a license
would formalise. A judge cannot dismiss "we would approach DTI" as naive when
there is a demonstrable, funded, government-operated network of exactly the
right counterparties, with a named provincial director and a published email.

**Be careful not to overclaim.** DTI is a government body, not a customer, and
this is not a partnership or a signed anything. The claim is *the channel exists,
is expanding, and is the obvious first conversation* — not that DTI has agreed
to anything.

*Source: DTI Region 7, October 2024.*

This is useful context in both directions: it shows the provincial government is

PLDT already do for free, and that argument is the published weights and the
scored output, not the price.**

## 2. Why they fail — the problem evidence

This is the core of judging criterion 1 (*clear problem statement with
supporting data*).

> **READ THIS BEFORE CITING ANYTHING IN THIS SECTION.** The international
> figures below are **US and global data about US small businesses.** They are
> useful for showing that a problem is *structurally* real, and they are fine if
> labelled as such. They are **not** evidence about Filipino businesses, and
> presenting them as Philippine evidence would be a misattribution a judge may
> catch. For genuinely Philippine evidence, use **§2a (ADB, UNIDO, DTI)** and
> **§2b (CPA Australia Philippines cut)** — those are local. See also the open
> item in §5.

**Cash flow is the leading cause of failure, in the US.** Multiple sources
converge on 82% of *US* small businesses failing due to poor cash flow
management or misunderstanding of cash (U.S. Bank study, widely cited via SCORE
and others).

- Forbes (2025-09-14) — "82 percent of businesses fail due to poor cash flow
  management": https://www.forbes.com/sites/melissahouston/2025/09/14/why-running-out-of-cash-is-the-1-reason-businesses-fail
- SCORE via PNC — "82% of all small businesses fail due to cash flow
  problems": https://www.pnc.com/insights/small-business/running-your-business/why-small-businesses-fail.html
- Preferred CFO (2026) breakdown: 82% poor cash flow skills, 79% started with too
  little money, 78% (other): https://preferredcfo.com/insights/cash-flow-reasons-small-businesses-fail-2026

**Ranking of causes — US small businesses** (useful for slide structure):
1. Cash flow problems (82%)
2. Insufficient capital at start (79%)
3. Insufficient demand for product/service (~35%) — US Chamber
4. Management/team problems (~20%)
5. Wrong marketing strategy (22%)

*Source: US Chamber of Commerce, 2025-04-23 —
https://www.uschamber.com/co/start/strategy/why-small-businesses-fail*

**Failure to plan / no business plan** is consistently listed first among
pre-emptive causes: NFIB states companies with a plan early stand the best
chance of success, and that "detailed market research and a thorough evaluation
of your competitors must be done during the planning process."

## 2a. Philippine failure and resilience evidence — **prefer these**

These are the local sources. Lead with them; use §2 above only as international
context.

**Cash flow fragility, measured in the Philippines.** An ADB survey of 3,877
MSMEs across developing Asia (1,804 of them Philippine) found:

- 70.6% of Philippine MSMEs temporarily closed during COVID-19 — the **worst
  rate among all countries surveyed**
- 58.8% reported **zero income**
- 36.7% had **no cash and no savings**
- 42.1% said their cash would run out **within a month**
- 12.7% applied for **bankruptcy**

https://www.rappler.com/business/adb-study-philippines-closures-layoffs-peers-coronavirus-pandemic

**DTI, 2020:** 90,000 businesses remained closed — about 6% of registered MSMEs
— from a survey of 3,000. https://business.inquirer.net/308391/dti-90000-businesses-remain-closed-amid-pandemic

**Almost no continuity planning.** Only **30% of micro and small enterprises had
business continuity plans**, and those plans were not responsive to the pandemic.
(UNIDO 2022, citing PSA.)

**The gap this reveals.** 42% of surveyed Filipino MSMEs said their cash would be
gone within a month, yet only 30% of micro and small enterprises had any
continuity plan at all. That is the specific, local, checkable gap the assessment
instrument addresses: knowing an area is weak *before* it becomes a crisis.
Note the caution in §5 — these are COVID-period figures, so do not present them
as a normal-year failure rate.

## 2b. Access to advice and finance — **strongest problem evidence**

**CPA Australia, 16th Annual Asia-Pacific Small Business Survey (May 2025),
4,236 SMEs across 11 markets.** The Philippines cut is unusually telling:

- Only **13% of Filipino SMEs sought advice from an IT consultant** in the past
  year, versus a **28% Asia-Pacific average** — the widest gap of any market
- **65%** of surveyed Filipino SMEs sought external financing in 2024, but only
  **22% found it easy to obtain**
- Of the Filipino SMEs that **did** invest in technology in 2024, **69%
  reported improved profitability** — above the 56% regional average

https://www.cpa.com.au/asia-pacific/small-business-survey

**Why this is the best evidence in the file.** Filipino MSMEs want business
advice and cannot easily get it — they are near the bottom of the region on
seeking expert help — and when they do adopt technology, it pays. A low-friction,
zero-cost, no-account way to get a structured read on the business addresses
precisely that gap, at a price point (free) that suits an owner who finds
external finance hard to obtain. It also gives a forward-looking argument rather
than only a failure argument.

**Philippine-specific stress evidence:**
- ADB survey of 3,877 MSMEs (1,804 Philippine): 70.6% of Philippine MSMEs
  temporarily closed during COVID-19 — worst among surveyed countries. 58.8%
  reported zero income; 36.7% had no cash and savings; 42.1% said cash would run
  out within a month; 12.7% applied for bankruptcy.
  https://www.rappler.com/business/adb-study-philippines-closures-layoffs-peers-coronavirus-pandemic
- DTI (2020): 90,000 businesses remained closed, ~6% of registered MSMEs, from a
  survey of 3,000. https://business.inquirer.net/308391/dti-90000-businesses-remain-closed-amid-pandemic
- Only **30% of micro and small enterprises had business continuity plans**, and
  those plans were not responsive to the pandemic. (UNIDO 2022, citing PSA.)

**How this connects to the tool.** The failure causes the research identifies —
cash flow blindness, no planning, no competitor evaluation, insufficient demand
testing — are precisely what the ten weighted categories in
`app/src/lib/scoring.ts` measure. The strongest categories by weight
(`customer-problem` 14%, `financial-viability` 14%, `market-opportunity` 12%)
map to the top causes of failure. That mapping is the argument, and it is
verifiable in the product.

### What competitors do NOT do

1. **Almost all are AI/LLM-generated reports.** No primary data, no structured
   scoring instrument, no reproducible weights. Output is prose.
2. **None are priced in Philippine pesos.** The whole tier ladder is $9–$199 USD.
   At roughly ₱58/USD, PHLAZE Pro+ is ~₱1,740/mo and Eye-O Consultant is
   ~₱11,500/mo — priced for a different market.
3. **None cite local Philippine data.** No PSA, DTI, or local MSME statistics.
4. **Almost none serve existing businesses.** They are pre-launch idea
   validators. PHLAZE (Business Canvas) and Eye-O (team assessment) come
   closest.
5. **Only PHLAZE claims local-first storage** — "All data stored locally on your
   device. No account required." That is a direct competitor to the offline,
   no-account positioning, and it is worth knowing before claiming uniqueness.

### Local / Philippine substitutes for software

The real competitor is not another app — it is **free human advice**:

- **DTI / SBDC** — government business advisory, largely free; MSME Caravan has
  trained 46,000+ owners. Access Partnership (2023):
  https://accesspartnership.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/philippines-economic-impact-exec-summary.pdf
- **Local consultants and CPAs** — bookkeeping/accounting firms typically offer
  a free 30-minute consultation, then hourly rates. Example: KG Consult Group
  states "Initial consultation for maximum of 30 minutes is always free,"
  thereafter hourly: https://kgconsult-ph.com/faqs
- **Facebook groups and peer advice** — high adoption, zero cost, no structure.

**Consulting cost anchor (international, for context only):** management
consultants typically run $100–$350+/hour, $3,000–$15,000+/month retainers
(Phoenix MI, 2026). Locally, PayScale lists the average Philippines consulting
base hourly rate at ₱174.96 (https://www.payscale.com/research/PH/Industry=Consulting/Hourly_Rate),
and Filipino freelancer guidance commonly anchors around ₱150–250/hour
(thebusinessmanual.ph, 2026). Human advisory is therefore **not cheap**
relative to local income.

**The gap this suggests.** No one appears to offer a structured, reproducible,
peso-priced assessment instrument that works offline with no account. That is
a defensible position — but it must be argued from the competitive evidence, not
assumed.

> **Important: the AI axis is closed.** Nine competitors above are AI-first, and
> earlier drafts of the submission leaned on that. The submitted app has **no AI
> feature** — see `AI-ANALYZER-DECISION.md`. We therefore do **not** compete on
> "adds AI." The defensible claim is narrower and stronger: *reproducible
> weighted scoring versus opaque prose generation, published weights versus a
> black box, local and offline versus cloud-dependent, and Philippine framing
> versus nine USD-priced tools with no local data.* Note that PHLAZE's local
> storage claim (§3) is about the same privacy property we hold — do not claim
> we are the only ones doing it.

## 3a. Where the money would come from — B2B/white-label

**Decision: license the tool to institutions, not to individual owners.**

Rationale, drawn from the evidence above:

1. **The owner is the wrong payer.** The CPA Australia data (§2b) shows 65% of
   Filipino SMEs sought external financing and only 22% found it easy to
   obtain. A micro enterprise with under ₱3M capital — the DTI Region 7
   definition of the segment most at risk (§1) — is not a good subscription
   customer. Asking them to pay monthly is asking them to spend money they have
   told a survey they struggle to get.
2. **Free human advice is the real competitor** (§3). We cannot out-compete DTI
   and SBDC counseling on price; we can make their counseling better. An
   institution that already advises MSMEs gets a structured, scored, printable
   instrument to run inside a session, at zero marginal cost.
3. **It resolves the offline-payment contradiction.** Our research flagged that
   offline/no-account design makes payment difficult. Under B2B that problem
   disappears — the institution pays, offline or not, and the owner never pays
   anything, which is also the strongest possible answer to the adoption risk in
   §4.
4. **It matches the competition's stated mission** (BYU Management Society,
   self-reliance): the tool strengthens local advisory capacity rather than
   replacing it.

**Target licensees, in priority order:**

1. **The 40 DTI Negosyo Centers operating in Cebu.** They already give free
   business advisory to exactly this segment, and the 40th — a DTI/Cebu City
   Government partnership — opened in May 2026. A scored, printable instrument
   costs them nothing and makes a 30-minute counseling session produce a
   consistent result. **This is the first conversation, and there is a named
   person to have it with** (DTI Cebu Provincial Director Marivic Aguilar).
2. **Cooperative and microfinance institutions** — already aggregate members and
   run business training; one relationship reaches many owners.
3. **SBDC and business incubators** — structured advisory capacity is the
   resource they lack.
4. **LGU micro-enterprise offices / Sugbo Negosyo Center** — the provincial
   program is already in the same channel.

**Settled 2026-10-01: pricing is left configurable, not fixed to a number.** The
policy is that each licensee sets a price in consideration of the businesses its
users are — a cooperative of micro-entrepreneurs, a city government and a
university incubator are not the same buyer, so one list price would be wrong for
at least two of them. No peso figure is committed anywhere.

**This is supported by the product, not a way of avoiding the question.** The
rate card is editable per deployer: `loadRateCard()` merges saved overrides onto
the shipped catalogue by product and tier (`app/src/lib/ratecard.ts`), and a
frozen proposal pins what was actually quoted. So configurable pricing is a
shipped feature that can be demonstrated on request.

**What still needs doing:** one clearly-labelled worked example for the Financials
slide, and the ₱150–250/hour anchor in §3 as the reference a licensee weighs a
licence against.

**A caution worth stating out loud.** Negosyo Center services are **free to the
MSME**. A government body whose mandate is free advisory is a hard sell, and
"we'd charge DTI" is not a credible plan. The realistic first step is probably
*free or near-free in exchange for a documented pilot* — which is an honest
position and one the handbook explicitly supports when framed as spending
prize money on a pilot (§6 of the working draft). Commercial licensing may
belong to the private institutions in points 2 and 3 rather than to DTI.

**Not to be presented as existing.** There is no license, no pilot, and no
institutional agreement. This is the model the entry will argue for, not a
business that trades today.

## 4. Digital adoption — the distribution problem

This matters because it determines both *reach* and *why an offline tool is
correct*.

**DTI Baseline Survey on Digitalization of MSMEs (400 MSMEs, NCR/III/IV-A):**
- 23% do not utilise ICT tools for business at all
- 51% are at Level 1 (basic only — email, Office, PC)
- Only 6% use purely online business or advanced tools (ERP, CRM, analytics)
- 26% are aware of government digitalization programmes
- MSMEs rate importance of digital innovation at 3.6/5

https://ecommerce.dti.gov.ph/madali/baseline_survey.html

**Adoption by tool type (Philippine MSMEs, estimated):**

| Tool | Adoption |
|---|---|
| Social media marketing | 80% |
| Digital payments | 70% |
| E-commerce | 65% |
| Mobile apps | 60% |
| Cybersecurity | 50% |
| Cloud computing | 45% |
| ERP systems | 30% |
| AI-based analytics | 25% |

*Source: "Digital Innovation and Entrepreneurial Success in the Philippine MSME
Sector," 2025 —
https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/eoik-2025-0056.pdf*

**Barriers:** financial constraints cited by 60% of respondents (highest severity,
4.8/5); regulatory issues 30%; cybersecurity concerns.

**DAI country brief (1,000 Philippine MSME owners/managers, 2022):** poor or no
internet connectivity was the most frequently reported difficulty in using
digital tools — 61% among online MSMEs. Offline MSMEs additionally reported
knowledge gaps and low confidence in using tools.
https://www.dai.com/uploads/final-msme-reports/philippines-country-brief.pdf

**Conclusion for the product.** Roughly half of MSMEs sit at the lowest
digital-maturity level, connectivity is a live barrier, and financial constraints
are the top barrier to adoption. Two consequences:
1. An **offline, no-account** product is not a limitation — it matches the market.
2. **Mobile-first** matters more than desktop. The web build already works;
   mobile reach is a distribution question worth answering.

## 5. Evidence gaps — research still needed

These are open. None are blocking, but each would strengthen the submission.

- [x] **The 82% cash-flow figure's provenance.** Resolved as a *framing* issue:
      it is **US data** and is now explicitly fenced as international context in
      §2, with Philippine evidence promoted into §2a/§2b. The underlying open
      question — a Philippine MSME failure-cause study — is separate and still
      open; see below.
- [ ] **Philippine MSME failure-cause study.** Still the biggest genuine gap.
      The failure-cause *rankings* in §2 are US/global. We have Philippine
      *stress* data (§2a: ADB, DTI, UNIDO) and Philippine *advice-access* data
      (§2b: CPA Australia), but no local study that ranks failure causes.
      **Mitigation:** argue from access and readiness rather than from a failure
      ranking. This is a legitimate and arguably stronger angle, and it does not
      require the missing study.
- [ ] **Actual business closure/survival statistics** for the Philippines in a
      **normal year.** The DTI and ADB figures are COVID-period. Do **not**
      present a pandemic closure rate as a normal-year failure rate — this is
      flagged twice because it is the easiest mistake to make and the most
      likely to be caught.
- [x] **Most recent PSA MSME statistics** — resolved. The **2023 Philippine
      MSME Statistics in Brief** (PSA List of Establishments) is confirmed as the
      authoritative source and gives Central Visayas directly: 87,986 MSMEs,
      88,468 total establishments. Also now resolved for §1's original ask: the
      2022→2023 regional growth is **+10.4%**. Still worth checking for a 2024
      or 2025 edition before you present.
- [ ] **A Cebu-Island-specific MSME count.** Not available in the public
      regional figures. Obtainable from PSA RSSO 7's provincial List of
      Establishments tables. **Do not estimate it** — see §1.
- [ ] **Willingness to pay.** No survey of what a Philippine MSME owner would
      pay for a business evaluation tool. All competitor prices are USD.
      **Deliberately not blocking** — pricing is left configurable per licensee
      (§3a), so the entry does not commit to a figure that would need this
      evidence. It is still the reason to run a survey before setting anything
      real. Note also that Negosyo Center advisory is free, which caps what a
      public-sector channel will pay at all (§3a).
- [ ] **Validate the "no local competitor" claim** by searching Filipino-language
      material, DTI-accredited consultants, and local startup directories.
      Still worth doing: PHLAZE claims local-first storage and is the closest
      thing to a direct competitor (§3).
